I am from Thrissur, Kerala in India. Pictured behind me are coconut trees, which I cannot climb (yet). Leslie Lamport and an education in computer science have clouded my vision, as a result of which I am unable to read things that are not LaTeX formatted.

I am a research assistant at Mark Humphries' Neural Data Science lab at the University of Nottingham, where I study algorithms underlying foraging decisions in rodents and humans.

Till September 2026, I was a research assistant at Ziad Hafed's lab in Tübingen, where I collected a large database of neurons from the macaque superior colliculus and primary visual cortex, and related the shapes of their spike waveforms to area-relevant physiology and function. This was a life-changing learning experience for me (I now like MATLAB).

In October 2024, I graduated with an M.Sc in Neural and Behavioral Sciences at the Graduate Training Center for Neuroscience, University of Tübingen, as an IMPRS MMFD scholar. I completed my thesis work at Andreas Nieder's lab, where I trained crows to perform a temporal wagering task to probe if they use confidence judgements when making leave/stay decisions.

I did my undergrad at Ashoka University, where I worked with Mahavir Jhawar and Subhashish Banerjee for my bachelor thesis on compression schemes that can incorporate deep probabilistic models of image data. While there, I was fortunate to work with Bittu Rajaraman and his neuroethology lab, where I studied call pattern production and recognition in bushcrickets endemic to the Western Ghats of India.

I want to understand the neural scaffolding of decision making in the brain, and in particular, how neuromodulatory substances affect them at the circuit level. In this vein, I am a huge proponent of comparative neurophysiology, and what it can potentially reveal about the ontogeny of complex cognition.
